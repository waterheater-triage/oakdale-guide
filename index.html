A Different County, a Different Permit Office

Most California jurisdictions require a plumbing permit to replace a water heater. In Oakdale:

  • Inside city limits: the City of Oakdale building division issues the permit.
  • Outside city limits with an Oakdale address: Stanislaus County handles it, not San Joaquin County.

Rural Properties and Well Water

Many homes around Oakdale are on larger lots, and some rural properties use private wells. Well water can be harder or carry more sediment than city water, and the quality can change over time. If you are on a well:

  • Flush a tank heater at least yearly, or more often if you see sediment in the drained water.
  • Consider a sediment filter ahead of the heater.
  • If you have a tankless unit, follow the descaling schedule closely.
  • Have the well water tested if hot water smells, discolors or leaves heavy scale.

Homes on city water can check the City of Oakdale's yearly water quality report for hardness.

Outbuildings and Long Pipe Runs

Larger properties sometimes have a heater serving a house far from the main bathroom, or a second heater in a shop or guest unit. Long runs mean longer waits for hot water. A professional can talk through options, such as a smaller point-of-use heater near a distant fixture, instead of simply replacing like-for-like.

Signs Your Heater Needs Attention

  • Water around the base
  • Rusty hot water, while cold runs clear
  • Popping or rumbling as it heats
  • A pilot that keeps going out
  • Hot water that runs out faster than it used to
  • A heater over 10 years old. The Department of Energy puts tank heater life at 10 to 15 years.

Repair or Replace?

A single failed part on a younger heater, such as a thermocouple, element or thermostat, is usually worth repairing. A leak from the tank means replacement. Between those, compare the repair price with a new installed unit, and ask for both options in writing.

Safety

PG&E supplies natural gas in the area. If you smell gas near the heater, leave the building on foot, then call 911 and PG&E from a safe distance. Keep the water heater at 120°F, as the U.S. Consumer Product Safety Commission recommends, and keep carbon monoxide alarms working if you have any gas appliance.
